**With Yuhang Pan and Wenda Zhang**

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract</b> (click to expand)</summary>
<br>
Difference-in-differences designs often measure treatment intensity as the distance between a unit's baseline outcome and a policy target. Below a common target, this dose is the baseline outcome with its sign reversed, so mean reversion alone can generate a positive dose response. Coding units already above the target as zero further inflates this response, while a signed dose does not identify variation associated with the target itself. We show that a two-sided spline nests both approaches and isolates target-specific variation through the kink at the target, although bounded outcomes can also generate mechanical kinks. Placebo targets using Chinese city data illustrate these issues and provide practical diagnostics for applied work.
