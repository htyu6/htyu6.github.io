<summary><b>Abstract</b> (click to expand)</summary>
<br>

South-facing apartments command a price premium in Chinese housing markets, but this premium is not fixed. Using 217,640 resale transactions in Shanghai from 2013 to 2020, we show that cumulative extreme-heat exposure during the listing period reduces the south-facing premium. The effect is concentrated in buildings with poorer thermal performance and is absent in the tallest buildings. The adjustment occurs primarily through sellers' listing prices rather than the listing-to-transaction discount. These findings show that the value of housing orientation is weather-dependent and varies with the thermal characteristics of the building.
